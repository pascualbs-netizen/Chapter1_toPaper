# Humanist Violence and Whitening: Animalization, Racialization, and the Coloniality of the Subject in *Lumpérica* and *Los pichiciegos*

> [!abstract] Thesis (locked — amend only via Vault_Constitution)
> Animalization tropes in Eltit's *Lumpérica* and Fogwill's *Los pichiciegos*
> do not merely represent dictatorial violence as a biopolitical state of
> exception. They expose the coloniality of power as the *prior*
> racialization that segments the modern human subject internally, pushing
> certain bodies toward the animal and designating them as preferential
> receivers of sacrificial violence. Fogwill dramatizes that sacrificial
> logic; Eltit explores the possibilities and limits of undoing it through
> an equally unstable cultural reappropriation.

## §II · Negros, Sheep, and Christians: The Sacrificial Chain in *Los pichiciegos* — budget ~2,500w · current: 0w

**Function:** The strongest close reading. Racialization → sacrificial war → desertion as re-animalization.

- [ ] **Spatial setup (~300w):** the *pichicera* — "visiones de una guerra
  subterránea"; horizontal, blind, faceless voices in the dark vs. the
  luminous surface where they are sheer fugitives. Self-animalization as
  desertion from national allegory.
- [ ] **The signifier *negro* (~600w):** Brecelli — "Si no fuera forro, no
  estaría aquí entre tantos negros roñosos como vos —decía Brecelli, que
  era porteño" (156). Provincial majority: "se veía raro que siendo que en
  el país la mayoría de la gente es porteña, allí la mayoría era de
  provincias" (156). *Cabezas negras* → Peronism → Ratier/Godillo
  (2 sentences). The bird-species origin of *cabecita negra*: racialization
  already animalization. Alberto & Elena on the class-slur's adaptive
  persistence (2 sentences).
- [ ] **Displacement onto "the Brazilian" (~300w):** "—Culear y ser
  brasilero. —Qué: ¿negro? —Cualquier cosa ¡Pero brasilero!" (99).
  Nationalist racism restores the pichis' whiteness; Argentina's
  self-whitening against its neighbors (Alberto & Elena).
- [ ] **The sacrificial chain (~700w):** the sheep/soldier mine juxtaposition —
  "El olor a oveja reventada por una mina es parecido al olor de cristiano
  reventado por una mina: olor a matadero…" (159). *Cristiano* as herd-faithful
  + one-sentence Freudian totem nod. The British officers as equals to the
  Argentine officers: "Con ellos son como iguales… toman el té juntos" (47).
  The chain closes: "entonces el argentino entiende lo que debió sentir
  aquella oveja que se iba yendo por el campo con tanto disimulo" (162).
- [ ] **Counter-reading (~200w):** Dowd's "indiscriminate force of modern
  warfare spares nothing and no one" (556) as foil — the novel's war is
  *acutely selective* in its distribution of violence.
- [ ] **Desertion as re-animalization (~400w):** escaping the chain by
  further animalizing; survival parasitic on sovereignty (reyes magos, el
  Turco's speculation, Zimmerman one sentence). They elude identification
  by undermining and parasitizing the symbolic — not by an exterior to it.

## §III · Quiltros and Fine Breeds: Ethnic Subjectivation and Its Capture in *Lumpérica* — budget ~2,500w · current: 0w

**Function:** The counter-movement — undoing racialization through unstable ethnic subjectivation, and its limits.

- [ ] **Spatial setup, condensed (~250w):** the *Luminoso* as naming/
  subjectifying law — "El letrero que se encenderá y se apagará, rítmico y
  ritual, en el proceso que en definitiva les dará la vida: su
  identificacion ciudadana" (Eltit 7). Kristeva/Lacan in TWO sentences
  maximum. The Nietzsche/Plato/Derrida excursus is CUT.
- [ ] **The *lumpérico animal*, brief (~350w):** the cow-mare-thing as
  subject-in-flux; its resistance ends in domestication ("Tordilla se
  muestra la yegua…"). ONE paragraph contrasting Braidotti's vitalist
  optimism: Eltit's novel denies the animal-becoming any guaranteed
  exteriority to capture. The Cabezón Cámara comparison is CUT (footnote at
  most).
- [ ] **The canine fable (~700w):** purebred dog on a leash vs. the
  *quilterío* — "puro callejeo." The interpellation: "Ladridos, ruidos que
  semejan / Quena o trutruca…" (86) — barking becomes Mapuche instruments,
  the leash becomes silver ornaments, the dog becomes *machi/Mater*.
  Re-ethnicization supersedes class/gender division: "La raza/ la raza
  encumbra sin el privilegio de las otras razas" (86).
- [ ] **The double edge (~500w):** the plaza transposed to the Altiplano,
  circular dance, "la bailan en rueda"; the electrification — "el pecho se
  I' electrifica la ruca asola" (86) — ethnic belonging attracting the
  dictatorship's violence (torture memory); the regime's double policy
  toward Mapuches (Araucano assimilation + land privatization).
- [ ] **Neoliberal capture (~400w):** Richards on Concertación
  multiculturalism — "the state has shaped (and disciplined) indigenous
  subjects and built racial inequality into the system" (18). The *toqui*
  and the dog both dance for the *amo*: "El toqui al señó/ al amo al
  trompa/ danza su trutruca en festival / La perra también amaestrada
  danza" (87). Intra-linguistic translation as pacification.
- [ ] **The final *quiltra* (~300w):** "la quiltra / sin collar de plata /
  ¿pa qué vaga?" (87). Ethnic identity as strategic, transitory passage —
  Spivak in ONE sentence. Not an essence but a destabilization.

## §IV · Reformulating the State of Exception — budget ~1,200w · current: 0w

**Function:** Synthesis + theoretical payoff + stakes for the field.

- [ ] **Synthesis (~300w):** Fogwill dramatizes how coloniality produces
  sacrificable subjects via racialization; Eltit explores undoing that
  operation via an equally unstable cultural reappropriation.
- [ ] **Theoretical payoff (~400w):** the zone of human/animal indistinction
  is not an undifferentiated suspension of law (Agamben) but the moment a
  *prior* hierarchization of the modern subject — organized by
  racialization — becomes visible. Animalization ceases to name a loss of
  humanity and reveals the mechanism by which humanity was always
  unequally distributed.
- [ ] **Stakes (~300w):** challenges (a) memory studies that treat the
  dictatorships as aberrations of the modern state, and (b) posthumanist
  animal studies that posit vitalist escape from biopolitics. The
  dictatorships are not exceptions to humanism but its fulfillment under
  colonial conditions.

  [[P06_So_What_Sharpener]].
