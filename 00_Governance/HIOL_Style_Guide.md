# HIOL Style Guide & Structural Analysis

> [!info] Source Corpus
> This guide is derived from close analysis of two published articles in
> *Hispanic Issues On Line* 28 (2022): "Contemporary Brazilian Cities,
> Cultures, and Resistance," ed. Sophia Beal and Gustavo Prieto.
> - Gibson, Annie McNeill. "Between the Poet and the Clown: The Poetic
>   Persona of Daniel Minchoni." pp. 125–139.
> - Alessandri Carlos, Ana Fani. "The Poiesis of the City and of
>   Resistance." Translated by Karina Mosling. pp. 32–46.

---

## 1. Structural Architecture

### Length & Pacing
- Articles run **13–15 printed pages** (approx. **6,000–8,000 words**
  including notes and Works Cited).
- Gibson: 15 pp. / Carlos: 15 pp. Both include substantial Works Cited
  (Gibson: ~20 sources; Carlos: ~12 sources).

### Opening Moves
- **Carlos** opens with an **epigraph** (a quote from Alessandro Buzo)
  followed by a disciplinary framing paragraph that situates geography
  within the Human Sciences before narrowing to the urban hypothesis.
- **Gibson** opens with a **contextual hook**: the reconfiguration of the
  Brazilian literary scene through *sarau* events, moving from the general
  cultural phenomenon to the specific case study (Daniel Minchoni) by the
  end of page two.
- **Pattern:** Broad cultural/spatial context → narrowing to the specific
  object of study → explicit statement of argument or hypothesis. Neither
  article buries its thesis.

### Section Headings
- Headings are **descriptive and thematic**, never generic ("Introduction,"
  "Conclusion").
  - Gibson: *Persona Poética/Poeta Palhaço*; *"Literatura ostentação"
    Performed at Elo da Corrente*
  - Carlos: *The Poiesis of the City*; *The Space of Poiesis in the
    Periphery of São Paulo*; *Literary Exhibitions and the "Pen" as a
    Weapon*; *The Communication Collective*; *The Periphery as a "Place
    for Speech" Is a "Place of Resistance"*; *Bringing It Together—Sharing
    Processes of Resistance*
- Headings often **bilingualize** or **theorize** key terms (e.g.,
  *Persona Poética/Poeta Palhaço*; *The Poiesis of the City*).
- Typical article: **4–6 substantive sections** plus Works Cited.

### Closing Moves
- Neither article uses a heading called "Conclusion."
- Carlos ends with a section titled *Bringing It Together—Sharing Processes
  of Resistance*, which restates the hypothesis and opens outward to a
  broader political project (Lefebvre's "possible-impossible").
- Gibson ends by returning to the primary text's social implications,
  quoting Antonio Candido on literature as citizenship, and closing on the
  sarau as "a profound and influential culture of resistance."
- **Pattern:** The conclusion **synthesizes** the argument, **reconnects**
  to the broader cultural/political stakes, and **gestures outward**
  without introducing new evidence.

### Notes
- Carlos uses **endnotes** (2 notes) for supplementary clarification.
- Gibson uses **no notes**; all clarification is woven into the prose.
- **Guideline:** Use endnotes sparingly, only for tangential scholarly
  context that would interrupt the argumentative flow.

## 2. Theoretical Integration

- Theory is **never siloed** into a standalone "Literature Review" or
  "Theoretical Framework" section.
- **Carlos** weaves Lefebvre (right to the city, implosion-explosion,
  possible-impossible), Djamila Ribeiro (*lugar de fala*), and Yúdice
  (performativity) directly into the spatial analysis of São Paulo's
  periphery. Theorists are introduced *at the moment their concept becomes
  operative*.
- **Gibson** introduces Peacock (clowning as play/otherness/failure),
  Candido (dialectic of malandroism), DaMatta, and Yúdice *in the act of
  analyzing Minchoni's performance*. Theory arrives as an interpretive
  tool, not as a preamble.
- **Pattern for the vault:** When drafting, introduce a theorist in the
  same paragraph where their concept is applied to the primary text.
  Never write more than one paragraph of "pure theory" without anchoring
  it to a textual example.

---

## 3. Treatment of Primary Sources

- **Extended quotation** is standard. Gibson quotes Minchoni's poetry and
  interview responses in full stanzas/paragraphs, always followed by
  analysis. Carlos quotes Vaz's *Cooperifa* manifesto and Lefebvre at
  length.
- Quotations are **never left to speak for themselves**. Every block quote
  or extended quotation is followed by at least one paragraph of
  interpretive commentary.
- **Pattern:** Quote → close reading → theoretical connection → transition
  to next point.

## 4. Multilingual & Translation Protocol

> [!important] Critical for English-language submission about
> Spanish/Portuguese-language texts.

- **Carlos's article is explicitly marked** "Translated by Karina
  Mosling." This signals that HIOL accepts and expects translated
  scholarship, but the translation must be credited.
- **Foreign terms** (Portuguese/Spanish) are **italicized** on first use
  and when used as technical or culturally specific concepts:
  - *sarau*, *malandragem*, *pedagoginga*, *lugar de fala*, *quilombo*,
    *funkeiros*, *baile funk*, *quiltros* (for our project)
- **Translations** are provided **immediately in parentheses** after the
  first occurrence:
  - Gibson: "*pedagoginga* … (the form, the didactics, the way of
    generating and transmitting knowledge…)"
  - Gibson: "*poeta-palhaço* (clown-poet)"
- **Block quotations in the original language** are followed by a **full
  English translation in parentheses**, as Gibson does with Minchoni's
  interview and poetry.
- **Proper nouns and place names** are not italicized (São Paulo,
  Pirituba, Malvinas).
- **Guideline for our project:** All Spanish terms (*pichiciegos*,
  *quiltros*, *lumperío*, *negro*, *sarau*) must be italicized on first
  use with a brief gloss. Subsequent uses remain italicized but do not
  require re-glossing. When quoting Fogwill or Eltit in Spanish, provide
  the English translation in parentheses immediately after.

## 5. Citation Format (MLA 9th Edition)

- **In-text:** (Author Page) — e.g., (Gibson 127), (Lefebvre 196–97),
  (Wynter 266).
- **Works Cited:** Alphabetical by author last name. Hanging indent.
  - Journal article:
    Author. "Title." *Journal*, vol. X, no. X, Year, pp. X–X.
  - Book:
    Author. *Title*. Publisher, Year.
  - Translated work:
    Author. *Title*. Translated by Translator, Publisher, Year.
  - Interview:
    Author. Personal interview. Date.
- **Self-citation at end of Works Cited:** Both articles include a final
  entry citing the article itself as it appears in the HIOL volume:
  - Gibson, Annie McNeill. "Between the Poet and the Clown…"
    *Hispanic Issues On Line* 28 (2022): 125–139.
- **Guideline:** Verify every citation against the actual source. The AI
  must **never** generate or guess a citation. If a source is not in the
  vault's `10_Context/` folder, flag it for manual verification.

## 6. Tone, Voice & Register

- **Authoritative but engaged.** Neither article is defensively
  over-cited. Claims are stated directly: "The hypothesis developed here
  is that…" (Carlos); "Minchoni employs techniques of humor… to redefine…"
  (Gibson).
- **First person is used sparingly and strategically.** Carlos: "I am
  referring to…"; "We must briefly consider…" Gibson avoids first person
  almost entirely.
- **Present tense for textual analysis:** "Minchoni *employs*," "the poem
  *appropriates* and *resignifies*," "the periphery *is* established."
- **No "dissertation-ese":** Neither article includes phrases like "as I
  will demonstrate," "it should be noted that," or "this chapter will
  argue." The argument is simply *made*.
- **Guideline for the vault:** Flag and suggest removal of any
  hedging, over-qualification, or meta-commentary about the writing
  process itself.

## 7. Thematic Alignment with HIOL Vol. 28

The analyzed volume centers on:
- **Center–periphery dynamics** and how marginal spaces challenge
  hegemonic cultural norms.
- **Embodied knowledge** and the body as a site of cultural production
  and resistance.
- **Resistance as *poiesis***: cultural production framed not merely as
  art but as a creative act of social transformation.
- **Spatial politics**: how urban, subterranean, or nocturnal spaces
  encode power relations.
- **Intersectionality**: race, class, and gender as co-constitutive
  axes of oppression and resistance.

> [!tip] Alignment Check for Our Project
> The friend's paper on *Lumpérica* and *Los pichiciegos* maps strongly
> onto these themes:
> - Subterranean space (*pichis* underground) ↔ Carlos's periphery
> - Nocturnal public plaza (*Lumpérica*) ↔ Gibson's *sarau* in the bar
> - Racialized bodies as sites of sacrificial violence ↔ intersectionality
> - Animalization as disruption of the "human" ↔ resistance as *poiesis*
>
> **Gap to address:** The friend's paper should explicitly engage with
> **spatiality and embodiment** as analytical categories, not just
> rhetorical tropes, to align with the volume's dominant framework.

## 8. AI Governance Rules (Vault-Specific)

> [!warning] These rules are binding for all AI interactions in this vault.

1. **No hallucinated citations.** Every reference must be traceable to a
   file in `10_Context/` or flagged for manual verification.
2. **Edit visibility.** AI suggestions use `%% AI: [suggestion] %%`
   comment syntax or appear in a separate blockquote. Never overwrite
   the user's prose directly.
3. **Dissertation-to-article shift.** Actively flag passages that read
   as "proof of comprehensive knowledge" and suggest condensation toward
   a single, sharp intervention.
4. **Language precision.** All Spanish/Portuguese terms must follow the
   protocol in §4 above. Diacritics must be correct.
5. **Theoretical anchoring.** Never allow more than one paragraph of
   unmoored theory. Every theoretical claim must be tied to a primary
   text example in the same or immediately following paragraph.
6. **Word count awareness.** Target 7,000–8,000 words for the final
   article. Flag sections that threaten to exceed this.
7. **English-language output.** All drafting, outlining, and revision
   suggestions are produced in English, even when analyzing
   Spanish-language source texts.
