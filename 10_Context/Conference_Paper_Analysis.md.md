# Conference Paper Analysis: Core Argument & Narrative Arc
**Original Title:** Violencia humanista y blanqueo: los animales en *Lumpérica* y *Los pichiciegos*
**Target Language for Article:** English

## 1. Central Thesis (The Intervention)

The paper argues that the animal figuration in Diamela Eltit’s *Lumpérica* and Rodolfo Fogwill’s *Los pichiciegos* does not merely represent the violence of the Southern Cone dictatorships as a biopolitical "state of exception" (à la Agamben). Instead, it reveals that this exception relies on the **coloniality of power**: a prior, constitutive racialization that internally segments the modern human subject. By pushing certain racialized bodies closer to the "animal," the modern humanist state designates them as preferential receivers of sacrificial violence. Ultimately, the novels use animalization not just to show subjugation, but to expose and disrupt the historical, unequal distribution of "humanity."

## 2. Key Supporting Points
1. **Critique of Agamben via Decoloniality:** The biopolitical suspension of human/animal distinctions never operates on an "undifferentiated" life. Drawing on Sylvia Wynter, Alexander Weheliye, and Aníbal Quijano, the paper argues that the "human" was always already stratified by European imperialist racial logic. 
2. **Fogwill’s *Los pichiciegos* (Sacrificial Logic & Flight):** In the context of the Malvinas War, Fogwill links the animalization of soldiers (as *pichiciegos*/armadillos and sheep) with the racialized class-insult "negro." The chain of substitution (negro -> argentino -> cristiano -> oveja) exposes the war as a sacrificial mechanism that reproduces coloniality. Here, animalization becomes a strategy of *flight/escape* from the sacrificial national subject.
3. **Eltit’s *Lumpérica* (Cultural Reappropriation & Instability):** In the context of the Chilean dictatorship, Eltit inverts Western metaphors of light/reason vs. dark/animal. In the "fable of the dogs," the purebred dog on a leash is juxtaposed with the *quiltros* (mutts) who use Mapuche instruments. This triggers an ethnic/cultural subjectivization (*machi-mater*) that destabilizes the racial order, though the novel also shows the risk of this identity being captured by the patriarchal/postcolonial state.
4. **Redefining the Human:** In both texts, animalization and racialization cease to be biological conditions and become the very sites where the modern definition of the "human" is disputed and renegotiated.

## 3. Theoretical Framework
*   **Giorgio Agamben:** State of exception, bare life, human/animal indistinction (used as the baseline to be critiqued).
*   **Sylvia Wynter:** "Overrepresentation" of the white, bourgeois European as the ideal human; degradation of the colonized to "less than human."
*   **Alexander Weheliye:** Cultural disciplining of *Homo sapiens* into human, not-quite-human, and non-human.
*   **Aníbal Quijano:** Coloniality of power.
