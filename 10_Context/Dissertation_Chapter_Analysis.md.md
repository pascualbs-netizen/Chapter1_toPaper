## 4. Proposed Article Outline (HIOL Format)

**Working Title:**
"Humanist Violence and Whitening: Animalization, Racialization, and the Coloniality of the Subject in *Lumpérica* and *Los pichiciegos*"

**Target:** 7,500 words | MLA 9th | English with Spanish terms italicized + glossed

---

### I. Opening (~800 words)
- **Hook:** *La Segunda* headline (1975): "Exterminados como ratones." One paragraph. The animalization of political enemies as state practice.
- **Argument:** Animalization tropes in Eltit and Fogwill do not merely represent dictatorial violence as biopolitical exception (Agamben). They expose the *coloniality of power* (Quijano) as the prior racialization that segments the human subject internally, designating certain bodies as preferential receivers of sacrificial violence.
- **Theoretical frame (condensed):** Wynter's "overrepresentation" + Weheliye's "cultural disciplining" in 2–3 sentences. Agamben's anthropological machine critiqued via Weheliye in 1 sentence.
- **Methodological note:** These novels have never been read together, nor from the figuración de lo animal. This reading connects the dictatorships to the longue durée of colonial history.

### II. "Negros, Sheep, and Christians: The Sacrificial Chain in *Los pichiciegos*" (~2,500 words)
- **Spatial setup:** The pichicera (underground cave) vs. the luminous surface of war. Self-animalization as desertion from national allegory.
- **The signifier "negro":** Brecelli's speech; provincial origin; cabezas negras → Peronism → Ratier → Godillo. The racialized class-insult as nation-building violence (Sarmiento's civilization/barbarism).
- **Displacement onto "the Brazilian":** The pichis' nationalist racism; Argentina's self-whitening (Alberto & Elena).
- **The sacrificial chain:** Sheep → Christian → Argentine → negro. The Malvinas war as sacrificial banquet (brief Freudian nod). The British officers as equals to Argentine officers.
- **Counter-reading:** Dowd's "indiscriminate force" vs. the novel's *selective* distribution of violence.
- **Desertion as re-animalization:** Escaping the chain by further animalizing; the pichis' survival as parasitic on sovereignty (reyes magos, the Turco's speculation). Not an exterior to capital/nation, but an undermining of its identificatory logic.

### III. "Quiltros and Fine Breeds: Ethnic Subjectivation and Its Capture in *Lumpérica*" (~2,500 words)
- **Spatial setup (condensed):** The Luminoso as symbolic/phallic law; the plaza as stage of identification; darkness as provisional shelter. 2–3 sentences on Kristeva/Lacan, no more.
- **The lumpérico animal (brief):** The cow-mare-thing as subject-in-flux; its resistance to domestication; its ultimate capture. ONE paragraph contrasting with Braidotti's vitalist optimism: "Unlike Braidotti's affirmative ethics, Eltit's novel denies the animal-becoming any guaranteed exteriority to capture."
- **The canine fable (4.6):** Purebred dog → quiltros → Mapuche instruments → machi-mater. The re-ethnicization that supersedes class/gender division.
- **The double edge:** The Altiplano transposition; circular temporality; the electrification (torture memory); the dictatorship's double policy toward Mapuches (Araucano assimilation + violence).
- **Neoliberal capture:** Richards on Concertación multiculturalism; the toqui dancing for the amo; intra-linguistic translation as pacification.
- **The final quiltra:** "la quiltra / sin collar de plata / ¿pa qué vaga?" Ethnic identity as strategic, transitory passage (Spivak, one sentence). Not an essence but a destabilization.

### IV. Conclusion: Reformulating the State of Exception (~1,200 words)
- **Synthesis:** Fogwill dramatizes how coloniality produces sacrificable subjects via racialization; Eltit explores the possibilities and limits of undoing that operation via cultural reappropriation.
- **Theoretical payoff:** The zone of human/animal indistinction is not an undifferentiated suspension of law (Agamben) but the moment when a *prior* hierarchization of the modern subject—organized by racialization—becomes visible.
- **The "So What?" for the field:** This reading challenges both memory studies (which treat the dictatorships as aberrations of the modern state) and posthumanist animal studies (which posit vitalist escape from biopolitics). The dictatorships are not exceptions to humanism but its *fulfillment* under colonial conditions.
- **Closing gesture:** Animalization ceases to name a loss of humanity and reveals the historical mechanism by which humanity was always unequally distributed.
